## What This Node Is

The PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire — 9 items) is the primary standardised measure for depression severity in IAPT and NHS Talking Therapies settings. It is administered at every session. Its clinical value is almost entirely in its trajectory across sessions — not in any single score.

> **The Trajectory is the Diagnostic.**
> A score of 12 coming down is a success signal. A score of 12 going up is a warning signal. The agent must never report a PHQ-9 score in isolation. It must always calculate and report the delta from both the assessment baseline and the previous session score.

---

## The Scoring Scale

The PHQ-9 asks nine questions, each rated 0–3:
- 0 = Not at all
- 1 = Several days
- 2 = More than half the days
- 3 = Nearly every day

**Total score range: 0–27**

| Score | Severity | Clinical Implication |
|-------|----------|----------------------|
| 0–4 | None / Minimal | Monitor only |
| 5–9 | Mild | Watchful waiting; low-intensity intervention |
| 10–14 | Moderate | **Clinical threshold — treatment indicated** |
| 15–19 | Moderately Severe | Active treatment required; review risk |
| 20–27 | Severe | Urgent clinical review; consider additional support |

---

## The Reliable Change Index (RCI)

A raw score change only becomes clinically meaningful when it exceeds the margin of measurement error. For the PHQ-9, the Reliable Change Index is approximately **5–6 points.**

| Change | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| < 5 points | Within measurement error — not reliable change |
| 5–6 points | Threshold of reliable change |
| > 6 points | Clinically significant improvement or deterioration |

> **Agent instruction:** When documenting PHQ-9 movement, note whether the change meets the RCI threshold. A drop from 14 to 12 is not reliable change. A drop from 19 to 12 is. The distinction matters for SOAP documentation and GP letters.

## Trajectory Analysis — The Core Clinical Skill

### The Snapshot Error
Reading a PHQ-9 score in isolation is the single most common misuse of this tool in IAPT settings. The number means almost nothing without direction of travel.

**Two clients, same score of 12 at Session 4:**

**Client A:** 19 → 16 → 14 → 12
- Responding to treatment. Consistent downward trajectory. 7-point drop exceeds RCI.
- Clinical response: Reinforce and persist. Show the client the graph. Use the data therapeutically — *"Look at this line. You feel stuck, but the data says you are moving. What specifically have you done differently in the last three weeks?"*
- Goal: Attribute change to client effort to build self-efficacy.

**Client B:** 8 → 9 → 11 → 12
- Deteriorating during treatment. Started mild, now moderate. The current intervention is failing or an unaddressed variable is driving the score up.
- Clinical response: Stop and pivot. Continuing the current approach is clinically negligent.
- Action: Conduct a meta-session. *"I've noticed your scores are creeping up over the last month. The plan we're using doesn't seem to be hitting the target. We need to pause and understand why."* Re-assess risk immediately. Review formulation for missed maintaining factors. Check homework adherence.

## The Two Delta Calculations

The agent must always calculate and report two numbers alongside the raw score:

**1. Assessment Delta** — Change from baseline assessment score to current score
- Formula: Assessment Score − Current Score
- Positive number = improvement
- Negative number = deterioration

**2. Session Delta** — Change from previous session score to current score
- Formula: Previous Score − Current Score
- Positive = improvement this session
- Negative = deterioration this session

**Example SOAP documentation:**
*"PHQ-9 this session: 12. Assessment baseline: 19. Assessment delta: −7 (reliable change met). Previous session: 14. Session delta: −2. Trajectory: consistently downward across four sessions."*

## Question 9 — The Override Item

Question 9 asks: *"Over the last two weeks, how often have you been bothered by thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting yourself in some way?"*

**Question 9 operates on completely separate clinical logic from the other eight items.**

A total PHQ-9 score of 5 with a score of 1 on Question 9 is more clinically urgent than a total score of 18 with a score of 0 on Question 9.

| Q9 Score | Response Required |
|----------|-------------------|
| 0 | No specific action beyond standard care |
| 1 (Several days) | Explore directly in session. Conduct full risk assessment. Document. |
| 2 (More than half the days) | Full risk assessment mandatory. Discuss safety plan. Consider supervisor consultation. |
| 3 (Nearly every day) | Urgent clinical review. Supervisor consultation required. Do not leave session without safety plan in place. |

> **Agent instruction:** If any Q9 score above 0 is detected in a transcript — whether stated verbally, extracted from a questionnaire, or implied by clinical content — this must be flagged immediately in the SOAP note as a priority item, regardless of total score. Flag in bold. This overrides all other documentation priorities. See [[risk_assessment]].

## Extracting PHQ-9 Scores From Audio Transcripts

In sessions where the PHQ-9 is administered verbally, the agent must be able to extract scores from conversational language rather than a completed form.

**Listen for:**
- Direct score statements: *"So your total today is fourteen"*
- Item-level responses: *"Nearly every day for the low energy, several days for the sleep"*
- Comparative statements: *"Your score is down from last week"*
- Client self-report of previous scores: *"I think I was a nineteen when we started"*

**When scores are partially stated:**
Document what is confirmed and flag what is missing. Do not estimate or interpolate scores. Example: *"PHQ-9 total not confirmed in session. Client referenced score being 'lower than last week.' Previous session score was 14. Full score unconfirmed — therapist to verify."*

## PHQ-9 in the SOAP Note

**Subjective section:**
Include the client's own language about mood this week. Do not replace this with the PHQ-9 score — they serve different functions. The score measures severity. The client's words capture experience.

**Objective section:**
Record the raw score, the assessment delta, the session delta, and the RCI status.
*"PHQ-9: 12/27. Assessment baseline: 19. Delta from baseline: −7 (RCI met). Delta from Session 3: −2."*

**Assessment section:**
Interpret the trajectory clinically.
*"Consistent downward trajectory across four sessions. Current score within moderate range but showing reliable change from moderately severe baseline. Treatment response indicated."*
or
*"Upward trajectory noted across three consecutive sessions (8→9→11→12). Deterioration exceeds RCI threshold. Formulation review and risk re-assessment indicated."*

**Plan section:**
Note any PHQ-9 driven clinical decisions — continuing current intervention, pivoting approach, escalating risk management, or scheduling supervisor consultation.

## Common Patterns and Their Clinical Meaning

**Early non-response (no RCI movement in first three sessions)**
Re-examine formulation. Is the maintenance cycle correctly identified? Is homework being completed? Is there an unaddressed life stressor?

**Score plateau (movement stalls mid-treatment)**
Common around the 10–12 range. Often signals a shift from behavioural to cognitive work is needed — BA has disrupted the withdrawal cycle but automatic thoughts are maintaining residual low mood. See [[automatic_thoughts_depression]].

**Score rises then drops sharply**
Sometimes indicates a crisis point followed by resolution. Document the context around the rise carefully — what was happening in that client's life? This data informs the relapse prevention work in final sessions.

**Score drops to below 5 before Session 8**
Clinically positive. Consider whether remaining sessions should shift focus to relapse prevention and maintenance rather than active symptom treatment. See [[ending_treatment]].

## What The Agent Should Do With PHQ-9 Data

1. **Extract the score** from the transcript — verbally stated, referenced comparatively, or confirmed by therapist
2. **Calculate both deltas** — from assessment baseline and from previous session
3. **Apply RCI threshold** — note whether change is reliable or within measurement error
4. **Check Question 9 first** — before any other interpretation, flag if Q9 > 0
5. **Classify the trajectory** — improving, plateauing, deteriorating, or insufficient data
6. **Document in SOAP Objective and Assessment** — raw score in Objective, clinical interpretation of trajectory in Assessment
7. **Flag clinical decisions in Plan** — any trajectory-driven change to treatment approach
8. **Never report score in isolation** — a number without trajectory is incomplete documentation
