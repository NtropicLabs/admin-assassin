## What This Node Is

Behavioural Activation (BA) is the primary intervention for depression in Brief CBT. It is not activity scheduling. It is a structured process of designing small behavioural experiments that test the depression's predictions against reality — generating evidence the client cannot easily dismiss.

---

## The Core Principle

**Action before motivation — always.**

Depression maintains itself partly through anticipatory predictions that feel like facts. BA treats every such prediction as a hypothesis to be tested, not an argument to be won. The therapist does not try to convince the client that things will get better. The therapist helps the client design an experiment that produces data.

> The activity is almost beside the point. What matters is the prediction that preceded it and the rating that follows it.

---

## The Four Components of a BA Conversation

A complete BA intervention in session requires four elements. Omitting any one of them significantly reduces clinical efficacy.

---

### 1. The Rationale (The Frame)
**Purpose:** Without this, the intervention feels arbitrary and patronising. With it, the client becomes a scientist running an experiment on their own depression rather than a patient being told what to do.

**Deliver this before selecting any activity:**

*"Before we choose what to do this week, I want to explain why I'm going to ask you to do something that might feel almost embarrassingly small. Depression is very good at making predictions — it tells you nothing will help, nothing will feel good, you won't manage it. What we're going to do is test that. Not argue with it, not ignore it — actually test it. So we're going to choose something small enough that you can definitely do it, write down what you predict you'll feel beforehand, and then compare that to what actually happened. We're collecting evidence. Does that make sense as a starting point?"*

**What this achieves:**
- Validates that the small scale is intentional, not insulting
- Pre-empts Minimisation of the Micro-Step before it arrives
- Transfers agency to the client — they are running the experiment

---

### 2. Collaborative Selection of a Graded Task
**Purpose:** To identify a specific, achievable activity calibrated to the client's current capacity — not their pre-depression capacity.

**The task must be:**
- **Specific:** Not *"clean up"* but *"wash three plates."* Not *"get some exercise"* but *"walk to the end of the street and back."*
- **Graded:** Achievable now, in the client's current depleted state
- **Binary-proof:** Defined clearly enough that there is no ambiguity about whether it was completed

**The Clinical Check:**
> If the client says *"I'll try to..."* — the task is too big. We need a task small enough that they can say *"I will."*

**Watch for and address:**
- [[false_binary_thinking]] — *"If I can't do the whole house, what's the point?"* Reframe partial action as complete success by design
- Identity Anchoring — *"This is nothing compared to what I used to do."* Unhitch the task from the past self entirely — see [[depression_maintenance_cycle]]

---

### 3. The Explicit Baseline Prediction
**Purpose:** This is the most commonly skipped step — and skipping it ruins the intervention. The prediction must be quantified and recorded before the activity occurs. Without a baseline, there is no experiment. Without an experiment, there is no data. Without data, the depression cannot be contradicted.

**In session, ask:**
- *"You've said it probably won't be worth it. On a scale of 0 to 10, how much Pleasure do you predict you'll get from washing those three plates?"*
- *"And how much Mastery — that sense of having done something — do you predict?"*

Write the numbers down visibly. If they say 0/10 — record 0/10. That is the hypothesis.

> The goal is Prediction Error — the measurable gap between what the depression forecast and what actually happened. Even a score of 2/10 against a prediction of 0/10 is clinically significant data. It is not nothing. It is proof that the depression lied.

---

### 4. Dual-Metric Tracking — Pleasure and Mastery
**Purpose:** To measure the actual experience after the activity, not just whether it was completed. Compliance tracking (*"did you do it?"*) is clinically useless on its own.

**Two dimensions only:**

| Metric | Definition | What It Detects |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Pleasure (P)** | Enjoyment, sensory satisfaction, positive feeling during or after | Depression's lie: *"You won't enjoy anything"* |
| **Mastery (M)** | Sense of accomplishment, control, or effectiveness | Depression's lie: *"You're incapable and useless"* |

**Why both metrics matter:**

Depression lies differently about pleasure and mastery. A client may feel 0/10 Pleasure from paying a bill but 6/10 Mastery. If only mood is tracked, the intervention appears to have failed. If Mastery is tracked, there is data contradicting the *"I'm useless"* narrative — even in the absence of enjoyment.

> Never collapse both into a single "mood" rating. The distinction between pleasure and mastery is the clinical precision that makes BA more than a wellness exercise.

**Timing:** Ratings must be recorded immediately after the activity — not retrospectively at the next session. Memory in depression is negatively biased. Retrospective rating will compress toward the depression's narrative.

## Common Clinical Complications

**Non-completion of the activity**
Do not treat this as failure. Treat it as data. Ask: *"What got in the way?"* The answer will almost always reveal a maintenance mechanism from [[depression_maintenance_cycle]] — usually Mood-First Fallacy or Predictive Apathy. This becomes the clinical target for the next session.

**Completion with low ratings**
A Pleasure score of 1/10 and Mastery of 2/10 against predictions of 0/10 is a successful experiment. Explicitly name the prediction error: *"You predicted zero. You got two. That gap is what we're interested in."*

**The client dismisses the ratings**
*"It was only a two, that's basically nothing."* This is Minimisation of the Micro-Step applied retrospectively — see [[depression_maintenance_cycle]]. Redirect to the prediction error, not the absolute number.

## What The Agent Should Do When It Detects BA In A Transcript

**1. Identify whether all four components were present**
- Was a rationale given?
- Was a specific graded task collaboratively selected?
- Was a baseline prediction recorded?
- Were both Pleasure and Mastery ratings tracked?

**2. Document in SOAP Plan**
Record the specific activity agreed, the baseline prediction if stated, and the rating system being used. If follow-up ratings from a previous session's BA task were reviewed, record those under Subjective with explicit notation of prediction vs actual.

**3. Flag incomplete BA**
If a task was set without a baseline prediction being recorded — flag this for therapist review. The experiment has no control condition without it.

**4. Extract Prediction Error data**
If ratings are mentioned in the transcript, calculate and note the prediction error explicitly. Example: *"Client predicted 0/10 Pleasure, reported 3/10 post-activity. Prediction error of +3 noted — contradicts anticipatory anhedonia."*

**5. Note trajectory across sessions**
BA ratings across multiple sessions form a recovery curve. Rising Mastery scores in the absence of Pleasure improvement is a common early pattern and is clinically significant — note it. See [[pleasure_mastery_ratings]] for full interpretation guidance.

---

## Linked Measures

**PHQ-9** — Track session by session alongside BA activity. Rising Mastery ratings should precede PHQ-9 improvement by one to two sessions. If BA is progressing but PHQ-9 is not moving, this warrants clinical review. See [[PHQ9_scoring]].

**Activity & Mood Diary** — The client-facing tool for recording activities, predictions, and P/M ratings between sessions. If mentioned in transcript, note whether it was completed, partially completed, or not attempted — and document any stated barriers.
