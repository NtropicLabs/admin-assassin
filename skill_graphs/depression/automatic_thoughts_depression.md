## What This Node Is

Automatic thoughts are immediate, involuntary internal reactions that occur in response to situations or events. They are the surface layer of the CBT cognitive architecture — fast, fleeting, situation-specific, and partially accessible to the client with gentle prompting. They are the primary target of intervention in Brief CBT.

---

## The Three-Level Cognitive Architecture

Understanding automatic thoughts requires understanding where they sit in relation to the deeper levels of cognition. Think of it as depth, not hierarchy.

| Level | Definition | Accessibility | Brief CBT Target? |
|-------|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Automatic Thoughts** | Immediate situational reactions | Partially conscious — quickly accessible | ✅ Primary target |
| **Intermediate Beliefs** | Rules, assumptions, and attitudes connecting core belief to automatic thought | Rarely conscious — emerges through pattern recognition | ✅ Secondary target — when patterns emerge naturally |
| **Core Beliefs** | Global, rigid beliefs about self, world, and others. Formed early through significant experience | Unconscious — felt as fact, not belief | ❌ Not targeted in Brief CBT |

### The Functional Relationship Between Levels

The three levels are not separate — they are a single operating system.

**Core Belief:** *"I am incompetent."*
**Intermediate Belief (the rule protecting it):** *"If people see that I'm not completely competent, I will be exposed as inadequate and rejected."*
**Automatic Thought (the alarm):** *"My manager probably thinks I'm useless."*

The intermediate belief exists to prevent the core belief from being confirmed. The automatic thought is the alarm that fires when the rule feels threatened. The behaviour exists to enforce the rule and avoid the feared outcome.

> The agent should understand this architecture because it determines intervention depth. In Brief CBT we work primarily at automatic thought level, with intermediate beliefs addressed only when they surface naturally through repeating patterns. Core beliefs are noted but not directly targeted.

## Identifying Automatic Thoughts in a Transcript

Automatic thoughts are not always stated directly. The client often reports the emotion or the behaviour without naming the thought. The agent should listen for:

- Emotional reactions that seem disproportionate to the described situation
- Statements that contain implicit predictions or assumptions
- Language that collapses interpretation and fact — *"He ignored me"* (fact) vs *"He thinks I'm worthless"* (interpretation stated as fact)
- Frequency words — always, never, everyone, no one, everything, nothing

## The Cognitive Distortions Taxonomy

Cognitive distortions are the specific patterns through which automatic thoughts become systematically inaccurate. Each one has a characteristic linguistic signature.

---

### 1. All-or-Nothing Thinking (Dichotomous Thinking)
**Mechanism:** Evaluates situations, self, or others in extreme binary categories. No middle ground exists. Anything short of perfect is total failure.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"If I don't get an A, I might as well have failed."*
- *"I ate a cookie so the whole diet is ruined."*
- *"It was a complete waste of time."*
- *"Everyone else has their life together — I'm the only one struggling."*

---

### 2. Overgeneralisation
**Mechanism:** Takes a single negative event and extrapolates it into a permanent, universal pattern of defeat. The specific becomes the universal.

**Transcript Signals (listen for frequency words):**
- *"I always mess things up."*
- *"This never works out for me."*
- *"People are always let down by me."*
- *"Just like every other time I tried."*

---

### 3. Mental Filter (Selective Abstraction)
**Mechanism:** Focuses exclusively on a single negative detail while ignoring the broader context. The positive is not rejected — it is simply not perceived.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"Did you see the look on his face when I stuttered? That ruined the whole presentation."*
- *"The whole day was a disaster because of that one email."*
- *"I can't stop thinking about that one comment."*

---

### 4. Disqualifying the Positive
**Mechanism:** Positive evidence is perceived but actively transformed into something neutral or negative. This is cognitive alchemy — turning gold into lead to maintain the negative belief.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"They only said that because they're being nice."*
- *"That was just a fluke — I couldn't do it again."*
- *"Whatever, anyone could have done that."*
- *"It doesn't count because you helped me."*

> **Agent Distinction — Mental Filter vs Disqualifying the Positive:**
> - **Mental Filter:** The compliments were not heard. *(Omission — the positive data never entered)*
> - **Disqualifying the Positive:** The compliments were heard but rejected as invalid. *(Active rejection — the positive data was neutralised)*
> The intervention differs. Mental Filter requires broadening attention. Disqualifying the Positive requires examining the reasoning used to dismiss the evidence.

---

### 5. Jumping to Conclusions
**Mechanism:** Drawing negative conclusions without sufficient evidence. Splits into two distinct subtypes.

#### 5a. Mind Reading
Assuming knowledge of another person's thoughts — always negative.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"My manager thinks I'm incompetent."*
- *"Everyone at the party could tell I was nervous."*
- *"She was judging me the whole time."*

#### 5b. Fortune Telling
Predicting a negative future outcome as though it were established fact.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"I knew I'd mess up the presentation."*
- *"There's no point applying — I won't get it."*
- *"It's just going to go wrong."*

> **Agent Note:** Mind Reading and Fortune Telling are the primary linguistic markers of the **Performance = Safety** intermediate belief. When both appear in the same transcript, flag the possibility of an underlying conditional assumption about competence and social evaluation. See [[intermediate_beliefs]].

---

### 6. Catastrophising and Magnification / Minimisation
**Mechanism:** Two sides of the same coin. Catastrophising escalates a negative event to its worst conceivable conclusion. Magnification/Minimisation inflates negatives and shrinks positives selectively.

**Transcript Signals — Catastrophising:**
- *"I made one mistake in that meeting — I'm going to get fired, lose the house, everything will fall apart."*
- *"If I fail this, my whole life is over."*
- *"This is an absolute disaster."*

**Transcript Signals — Magnification/Minimisation:**
- *"That presentation going badly has ruined everything."* (Magnification of negative)
- *"Getting promoted was nothing really, anyone could have done it."* (Minimisation of positive)

---

### 7. Emotional Reasoning
**Mechanism:** Treats feelings as evidence about reality. Because something feels true, it must be true. This is the anti-CBT distortion — it collapses the distinction between emotion and fact that CBT depends on.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"I feel like an idiot, so I must be one."*
- *"I feel guilty, so I must have done something wrong."*
- *"I feel overwhelmed, so the problem must be impossible."*
- *"If I was actually good at my job, I wouldn't feel this anxious."*

> **Clinical note:** Emotional Reasoning is particularly resistant to direct challenge because the evidence the client is citing is their own felt experience, which is real. The intervention is not to dispute the feeling but to separate it from the conclusion — *"The feeling is real. What we're examining is whether the feeling is a reliable guide to the facts."*

---

### 8. Should Statements (The Tyranny of Shoulds)
**Mechanism:** Rigid internal rules about how the self must perform, feel, or behave. Violation generates guilt. When applied to others, generates resentment.

**Transcript Signals (listen for modal verbs):**
- *"I should be over this by now."*
- *"I shouldn't need help with this."*
- *"I must not make mistakes."*
- *"I ought to be able to handle this on my own."*

> **Agent Note:** Should Statements are frequently the linguistic surface of an intermediate belief. Repeated Should Statements across sessions — especially across different situations — indicate a rigid underlying rule worth naming. See [[intermediate_beliefs]].

---

### 9. Labelling and Mislabelling
**Mechanism:** An extreme form of overgeneralisation. Rather than describing a specific error or behaviour, the client fuses it with their identity. The behaviour becomes the self.

**Transcript Signals (identity fusion language):**
- *"I'm a loser."* (vs *"I lost"*)
- *"I'm a failure."* (vs *"I failed at this task"*)
- *"I'm such a burden."*
- *"I'm just a lazy person."*

> **Agent Note:** Labelling is a direct linguistic marker of core belief activation. When detected, note in SOAP Assessment that the client is operating at identity level. This does not change the intervention in Brief CBT — we still work at automatic thought level — but it flags severity and potential treatment complexity.

---

### 10. Personalisation
**Mechanism:** Attributing negative external events to oneself as primary cause, regardless of other contributing factors.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"The dinner party was quiet — it must be because I was boring."*
- *"My son failed his test. I'm a bad mother."*
- *"If I'd been more supportive, he wouldn't have been in a bad mood."*

---

## The Clinical Boundary — When Brief CBT Is Working vs When It Isn't

In Brief CBT, automatic thought level work should produce visible movement within three to four sessions. The signal that it's working: the client begins to catch thoughts themselves between sessions, alternative perspectives become accessible, and PHQ-9 scores show directional movement.

**The signal that something deeper is indicated:**

The same automatic thoughts reappear session after session without shift. Thought challenging produces intellectual agreement but no emotional change. *"I know it's not rational but I still feel it completely."* The client engages with every intervention correctly but nothing moves.

This pattern suggests the automatic thoughts are being continuously regenerated by a core belief that Brief CBT is not reaching. This is not treatment failure — it is a clinical signal. Document it clearly and flag for supervisor review. The client may need longer-term work or a different modality.

> The agent should flag this pattern if the same distortion categories appear across three or more sessions without any noted shift in the client's response to them.

## What The Agent Should Do When It Detects Automatic Thoughts

**1. Identify and categorise**
Label each detected automatic thought with its specific distortion type from the taxonomy above. Do not use generic labels like "negative thinking."

**2. Note the level**
Is this an automatic thought, or does the surrounding context suggest an intermediate belief is visible? If the same theme appears multiple times in the transcript, note the possible rule operating underneath.

**3. Document in SOAP Subjective**
Quote the client's actual words. Then label: *"Consistent with [distortion type]. Suggests [intermediate belief] may be active."*

**4. Flag unaddressed distortions for SOAP Plan**
If a significant distortion was identified in the transcript but not directly addressed by the therapist, flag it as an unaddressed cognitive target for the next session.

**5. Track pattern across sessions**
If the same distortion categories appear repeatedly, note the frequency. Three or more sessions of the same unchallenged pattern is a clinical flag — see boundary note above.
