## What This Node Is

The Depression Maintenance Cycle is a self-reinforcing feedback loop in which low mood drives behavioural withdrawal, which removes sources of positive reinforcement (pleasure and mastery), which deepens the low mood — accelerating the cycle with each iteration.

---

## The Core Loop

1. **Trigger / Mood Drop** — An event or internal state causes an initial drop in mood and energy.
2. **Behavioural Withdrawal** — The client reduces activity, social contact, and responsibility in response to the mood drop.
3. **Loss of Reinforcement** — Withdrawal eliminates the primary sources of pleasure and mastery from the client's daily life.
4. **Deepening Mood** — The absence of positive experience confirms negative beliefs about the self and the world, worsening mood and making further action feel increasingly impossible.

> The cycle is not linear — it is recursive and accelerating. Each revolution makes the next one faster.

---

## What It Sounds Like In A Transcript

The cognitive layer of the depression maintenance cycle functions as a **permission structure for withdrawal**. These are not simply "negative thoughts" — they are functional cognitions that make inaction feel logical, rational, and even necessary to the client.

The agent must listen for five distinct categories of permission-giving cognition:

---

### 1. The Mood-First Fallacy
**Function:** Reverses the causal engine. The client believes that feeling better is a prerequisite for action, rather than a result of it. This is the single most common linguistic marker of the maintenance cycle.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"I'm just going to wait until I have the energy to do it right."*
- *"I'll tackle that when I'm feeling a bit more like myself."*
- *"I don't want to force it if it doesn't feel natural."*
- *"I need to rest up this week so I can be productive next week."*

---

### 2. Minimisation of the Micro-Step
**Function:** Devalues the graded activity to justify inaction. Frames the clinical intervention as trivial, patronising, or disproportionate to the presenting problem.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"It seems stupid to put 'brush teeth' on a list. I'm an adult."*
- *"How is walking to the mailbox going to fix the fact that I lost my job?"*
- *"That doesn't even count as doing something."*
- *"It's a drop in the bucket. It won't make a dent."*

---

### 3. Predictive Apathy (Anticipatory Anhedonia)
**Function:** Treats a negative prediction about future experience as an established fact. The client forecloses the possibility of reward before the activity has occurred, eliminating any motivation to test the prediction behaviourally.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"I know I'm just going to sit there and be miserable, so why go?"*
- *"It's just going to be exhausting."*
- *"I won't get anything out of it."*
- *"It's just going to remind me of how much I used to love this — and that will hurt."*

---

### 4. Identity Anchoring
**Function:** Compares the client's current capacity to their pre-depression baseline, generating shame and making low-demand activity feel like an admission of failure rather than a clinical step forward.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"I used to run 10k every morning — walking for five minutes is pathetic."*
- *"This isn't me. I'm a high performer."*
- *"I'm just lazy. I've turned into a lazy person."*

> **Clinical note:** Identity Anchoring is particularly insidious because it sounds like self-awareness. The client appears to be accurately describing themselves. The therapist's task is to gently separate current capacity from permanent identity — this is a temporary state, not a character truth.

---

### 5. The False Binary
**Function:** All-or-nothing thinking applied specifically to effort thresholds. An implicit rule that partial action carries zero value — making graded activity feel irrational or pointless on its own terms, independent of past identity.

**Transcript Signals:**
- *"If I can't do the whole house, I'm not touching any of it."*
- *"Doing half the assignment is the same as failing."*
- *"What's the point of calling for five minutes if we usually talk for an hour?"*

> **Distinguish from Identity Anchoring:** Identity Anchoring compares to the past self. The False Binary applies a zero-sum rule about effort in the present. The intervention is different — one requires unhooking from identity, the other requires challenging the all-or-nothing belief directly.

---

## The Clinical Error To Avoid

**The Gym Trap.**

This occurs when the therapist — consciously or unconsciously — anchors activity goals to the client's pre-depression baseline. Hearing *"I used to go to the gym four times a week"* and working towards reinstating that goal guarantees failure, because the client's current physiological and cognitive capacity is genuinely compromised.

Worse, it keeps the client measuring every small action against who they were. Even a successful low-demand activity becomes evidence of loss rather than progress.

Activity must be completely unhitched from identity and from the past self. The clinical frame is not *"getting back to normal"* — it is *"generating one small piece of new evidence."*

---

## Intervention Principle

**Action before motivation — always.**

We do not wait for mood to improve before changing behaviour. We change behaviour to change mood. Motivation in depression follows action; it does not precede it.

Interventions must be:
- **Graded** — calibrated to current capacity, not past capacity
- **Collaboratively chosen** — client selects the activity, which increases follow-through
- **Tracked** — rated on Pleasure (0-10) and Mastery (0-10) after completion, not before

The clinical target is not enjoyment. It is the generation of counter-evidence to the depression's narrative.

---

## What The Agent Should Do When It Detects This

**1. Re-label beyond "negative thought"**
Do not categorise these statements as generic negative thinking or low mood. Flag them explicitly as **Maintenance Mechanisms** — functional cognitions that actively sustain the cycle.

**2. Extract the specific category**
Identify which permission structure is present:
- Waiting for energy? → **Mood-First Fallacy**
- Devaluing the task? → **Minimisation of Micro-Step**
- Forecasting no reward? → **Predictive Apathy**
- Comparing to past self? → **Identity Anchoring**
- All-or-nothing effort rule? → **False Binary**

**3. Document in SOAP Assessment**
In the Assessment section, note: *"Cognitive layer actively reinforcing behavioural withdrawal."* Quote the specific client phrase as evidence of the maintenance mechanism, not merely as evidence of low mood.

**4. Flag for SOAP Plan**
If a maintenance mechanism is present but was not addressed in session, flag this for the therapist's review. It represents an unaddressed clinical target.

## Linked Measures

**PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire — 9 items)**

| Score | Severity |
|-------|----------|
| 0–4 | None / Minimal |
| 5–9 | Mild |
| 10–14 | Moderate *(clinical threshold — treatment likely indicated)* |
| 15–19 | Moderately Severe |
| 20–27 | Severe |

> **Critical clinical note for the agent:** A single PHQ-9 score is a snapshot. **Trajectory matters more than any individual score.** A score stuck at 14 across four sessions is a different clinical picture from a score that has moved from 19 to 14 in the same period. The agent should note direction of travel, not just the number.
