## What This Node Is

A thought record is a structured written tool that externalises the internal dialogue of depression so the client can examine it from outside rather than being inside it. It is the primary vehicle for cognitive restructuring in CBT.

It is not a positive thinking exercise. It is a formalised experiment in perspective-taking — moving the client from immersion in a thought to examination of it.

> **The paper does the holding.** The client doesn't have to keep the entire cognitive sequence in their head while simultaneously trying to evaluate it. The structure creates the distance that makes evaluation possible.

---

## The Two Formats

Thought records come in two formats used at different stages of treatment. Using the wrong format at the wrong stage is a common clinical error.

---

### The Five-Column Record — The Investigation Phase

Used in **Sessions 2–4** approximately. Introduced when the client has understood the CBT model but before they have sufficient evidence-gathering skill to generate credible alternatives.

| Column | Label | Clinical Purpose |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Situation** | The trigger — who, what, where, when. Factual only. |
| 2 | **Mood** | One word + intensity (0–100%). The alarm signal. |
| 3 | **Automatic Thought** | *"What was going through my mind just then?"* + Belief rating (0–100%) |
| 4 | **Evidence For** | Why does this thought feel true? Facts only — not feelings. |
| 5 | **Evidence Against** | What facts contradict this thought? The hardest column. |

**Why five columns first — The Detective Before the Judge**

Adding the Balanced Thought (Column 6) and Mood Re-Rating (Column 7) too early asks the client to render a verdict before they have gathered sufficient evidence. The result is forced positivity — a rational-sounding alternative that carries zero emotional weight because it hasn't been earned.

The five-column record restricts the task to observation and investigation. It says to the client: *"You don't have to change your mind yet. You just have to put the evidence on the table."*

This separation is clinically essential. Teach the client to be a Detective first. Once they can reliably generate Evidence Against without prompting, they are ready to become the Judge.

> **Forced positivity signal:** If a client's Column 6 balanced thought sounds logical but their mood re-rating shows minimal shift — they were performing the task rather than doing it. The five-column phase was skipped too quickly. Return to evidence gathering.

---

### The Seven-Column Record — The Synthesis Phase

Used from **Session 4–5 onwards**, once the client can reliably generate Evidence Against. The seven-column version adds the synthesis and outcome measurement that make cognitive restructuring clinically complete.

| Column | Label | Clinical Purpose |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Situation** | The trigger — who, what, where, when |
| 2 | **Mood** | One word + intensity (0–100%) |
| 3 | **Automatic Thought** | The interpretation + belief rating (0–100%) |
| 4 | **Evidence For** | Facts supporting the thought |
| 5 | **Evidence Against** | Facts contradicting the thought |
| 6 | **Balanced Thought** | Synthesis of columns 4 and 5 — not positive thinking, accurate thinking + belief rating (0–100%) |
| 7 | **Re-Rate Mood** | Same emotion word + new intensity (0–100%) |

---

## Column 6 — The Balanced Thought

This is the most misunderstood column in the entire thought record.

A balanced thought is not the opposite of the automatic thought. It is not optimistic. It is not reassuring. It is the most accurate statement the client can make given all the available evidence — positive, negative, and neutral.

**Automatic thought:** *"I'm a complete failure."*
**Forced positive (wrong):** *"I'm actually great and doing really well."*
**Balanced thought (correct):** *"I made a significant mistake in this situation. I have also succeeded in comparable situations before. One outcome doesn't determine my overall capability."*

The balanced thought should feel slightly uncomfortable to write. If it feels easy and pleasant, it's probably not accurate — it's probably reassurance.

> **Belief rating on Column 6 matters.** Ask the client to rate how much they believe the balanced thought (0–100%). A balanced thought rated at 20% belief will produce minimal emotional shift. The evidence gathering in Column 5 was insufficient. More evidence is needed before synthesis.

---

## Column 7 — The Emotional Delta

**This is the only column that tells you whether the cognitive work actually landed.**

Re-rating the mood after completing the record provides the clinical evidence of whether a genuine cognitive shift occurred or merely an intellectual one.

### The Three Outcomes:

**Significant Drop (>20 percentage points)**
*Mood: Sad 90% → Sad 55%*

Validated cognitive shift. The evidence gathering produced genuine perspective change. The intervention worked. Note: the goal is never to eliminate the emotion — it is to reduce its intensity to a proportionate level.

**Minimal Drop (<10 percentage points)**
*Mood: Sad 90% → Sad 82%*

Intellectual insight only. The client generated a balanced thought that sounds correct but carries no emotional weight. This is the **Head-Heart Discrepancy** — knowing something logically without feeling it emotionally.

Clinical causes:
- The evidence against (Column 5) wasn't personally meaningful — too abstract or generic
- The automatic thought being worked on wasn't the hot thought — the real driver is still untouched underneath
- The client's belief rating on the balanced thought (Column 6) is very low

**No Drop or Increase**
*Mood: Sad 90% → Sad 90% or higher*

The thought record has not engaged the maintaining cognition at all. Either the wrong thought was targeted, the evidence was insufficient, or an intermediate belief is actively regenerating the automatic thought faster than the record can address it. See [[intermediate_beliefs]].

> **The Head-Heart Discrepancy is a clinical signal, not a treatment failure.** It tells you precisely where to go next — deeper into the evidence, or to the belief underneath the thought.

## Completing a Thought Record in Session

The first thought record should always be completed collaboratively in session — not assigned as homework and returned blank or incorrectly completed.

**The Socratic approach:**

Column 1 — Situation: *"Walk me through exactly what was happening. Where were you? What time was it? Who else was there?"* Ground it in concrete detail.

Column 2 — Mood: *"What emotion came up? Just one word if possible. And if 100% is the most intense you've ever felt that, where was it on that day?"*

Column 3 — Automatic Thought: *"What went through your mind in that moment? Not what you thought later — what was the first thought?"* + *"How much did you believe it in that moment, 0 to 100?"*

Column 4 — Evidence For: *"What makes this thought feel true? I'm looking for facts here — things that actually happened, things someone said. Not feelings."*

Column 5 — Evidence Against: *"Now — what are the facts that don't fit this thought? Even small ones count."* This column will often need prompting. *"Has there ever been a time when the opposite was true?"*

Column 6 — Balanced Thought: *"Given everything in columns 4 and 5 — both sides — what's the most accurate statement you can make? Not the kindest. The most accurate."*

Column 7 — Re-rate: *"Now look back at that emotion in column 2. Same word — where is it now, 0 to 100?"*

## What a Completed Thought Record Tells the Agent About Treatment Stage

The format and quality of thought records present in a transcript are diagnostic indicators of where the client is in the treatment process.

| What the Agent Sees | What It Means |
|---------------------|---------------|
| No thought records mentioned | Early sessions (1–2) or homework non-completion — note and flag |
| Five-column record referenced | Sessions 2–4, evidence-gathering phase, client learning to identify thoughts |
| Seven-column record referenced | Sessions 4+, synthesis phase, client has evidence-gathering skill |
| Balanced thought with low belief rating | Insufficient evidence — Column 5 needs strengthening |
| Minimal emotional delta (<10%) | Head-Heart Discrepancy — flag for Socratic deepening or intermediate belief exploration |
| Significant emotional delta (>20%) | Genuine cognitive shift — document as therapeutic progress in SOAP Assessment |
| Same thought appearing across multiple records | Intermediate belief active — pattern warrants naming. See [[intermediate_beliefs]] |

## The Hot Thought Identification Problem

Not every automatic thought identified in Column 3 is the right thought to work on.

The hot thought is the thought carrying the most emotional charge — the one most directly connected to the mood in Column 2. It is often not the first thought the client names. Clients frequently offer a safer, more surface-level thought first.

**Signal that Column 3 isn't the hot thought:**
- Belief rating on Column 3 is below 70%
- Working through columns 4–6 produces minimal emotional delta
- The client seems disengaged or mechanical during the record

**Clinical move:** *"We've worked through that thought and your mood has barely shifted. I wonder if there's a thought underneath that one that's carrying more weight. When you were in that situation, was there something even more distressing going through your mind?"*

> **Agent instruction:** If a completed thought record shows a minimal emotional delta, flag the possibility that the hot thought was not correctly identified. Note in SOAP Assessment: *"Thought record completed. Emotional delta minimal — possible hot thought identification issue. Recommend Socratic exploration of Column 3 in next session."*

## Clinical Errors to Avoid

**Assigning thought records as first homework**
The first record must be completed in session. A client sent away to complete one independently before they understand the structure will return with a blank sheet or a record that conflates thoughts with feelings throughout.

**Accepting feelings as evidence**
Column 4 must contain facts. *"I felt stupid"* is not evidence that the client is stupid. *"I gave an incorrect answer in the meeting"* is evidence. The distinction must be taught explicitly and enforced gently.

**Moving to Column 6 too quickly**
If Column 5 is thin — one or two weak pieces of evidence — the balanced thought will not hold. Spend more time in Column 5 before synthesising. Ask: *"What else? Has there ever been a time when...? What would you say to a friend who had this thought?"*

**Treating the thought record as a form to fill in**
The thought record is a conversation scaffold, not an administrative task. If the client is filling it in mechanically without engagement, the intervention has become an exercise in compliance rather than a genuine cognitive examination.

## What The Agent Should Do When It Detects a Thought Record

**1. Identify the format**
Five-column or seven-column — note which is being used and whether it is appropriate to the session number.

**2. Extract the key data points**
- Situation described
- Mood word and initial intensity rating
- Automatic thought and belief rating
- Whether evidence for and against were generated
- Balanced thought and belief rating (if seven-column)
- Mood re-rating and emotional delta (if seven-column)

**3. Calculate the emotional delta**
Compare Column 2 mood rating to Column 7 mood rating. Classify as significant shift, minimal shift, or no shift.

**4. Apply the Head-Heart Discrepancy rule**
If delta is minimal — flag in SOAP Assessment. Note possible causes: insufficient evidence, wrong thought targeted, intermediate belief active.

**5. Check for repeated thoughts**
If the same or similar automatic thought appears across multiple session references, flag as possible intermediate belief. See [[intermediate_beliefs]].

**6. Document in SOAP**
- **Subjective:** Client's reported experience of completing the record
- **Objective:** Format used, thought targeted, emotional delta
- **Assessment:** Quality of cognitive shift, any Head-Heart Discrepancy noted
- **Plan:** Whether to continue five-column or progress to seven-column; any Column 5 strengthening needed; hot thought clarification if indicated
