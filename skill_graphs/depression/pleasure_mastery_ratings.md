## What This Node Is

A dual-metric scoring system (0–10) used to quantify the subjective experience of graded activity. It is the primary data-collection tool for Behavioural Activation. It transforms vague retrospective feedback (*"it was okay"*) into clinical data (*"Pleasure: 1, Mastery: 4"*) that can be tracked, compared, and used as evidence against the depression's predictions.

---

## The Two Metrics

Depression affects pleasure and mastery differently. They must always be tracked separately — collapsing them into a single "mood" rating loses the clinical precision that makes BA more than a wellness exercise.

### Pleasure (P) — 0 to 10
**Definition:** Enjoyment, sensory satisfaction, fun, or relaxation experienced during or after the activity.

**The barrier:** Anhedonia — the inability to feel joy. In moderate to severe depression, pleasure scores often remain near zero even as activity increases. This is expected and does not indicate treatment failure.

**Examples of pleasure-generating activities:** Eating a favourite meal. A warm bath. Listening to music. Time with a pet.

---

### Mastery (M) — 0 to 10
**Definition:** A sense of accomplishment, competence, effectiveness, or control — independent of enjoyment.

**The barrier:** Learned helplessness — the belief that one's actions have no impact on outcomes.

**Examples of mastery-generating activities:** Paying a bill. Cleaning a single shelf. Sending a difficult email. Getting out of bed before noon.

> **Clinical nuance:** High mastery tasks are frequently low pleasure. Doing taxes produces zero enjoyment and genuine competence. If the therapist only asks *"did you enjoy it?"* the data says failure. If they ask *"did you feel effective?"* the data shows a win. The distinction is everything.

---

## The "Mastery First" Recovery Pattern

**Clinical Rule: Mastery typically recovers before Pleasure.**

The sense of agency — *"I can do this"* — returns before the sense of enjoyment — *"I like doing this."* This is a consistent and well-recognised pattern in depression recovery that the agent must understand to interpret data correctly.

**The signal:** Client reports P: 0/10, M: 3/10.

**Correct interpretation:** This is not a failed intervention. It is the engine restarting. The cognitive system is beginning to acknowledge capability even while the emotional system remains offline. Mastery improvement without pleasure improvement is early-stage recovery — expect it, name it, use it therapeutically.

**Incorrect interpretation:** *"The client didn't enjoy it, so the task didn't help."* This reading collapses both metrics into pleasure and misses the clinical movement entirely.

> **Agent instruction:** Never evaluate BA success on Pleasure alone. A session where the client reports P: 0 and M: 5 is clinically productive. Validating the Mastery explicitly is how Pleasure is eventually unlocked.

## The Prediction as Clinical Signal

The score the client estimates before the activity is not just preparation — it is a direct measure of the depression's cognitive grip. It must be recorded before the activity occurs and compared to the actual score afterwards.

### What predictions reveal:

**Prediction of P: 0 / M: 0**
The maintenance cycle is fully dominant. The client believes action is completely futile — no enjoyment and no competence anticipated. Full Predictive Apathy active. See [[depression_maintenance_cycle]].

Clinical response: Validate the prediction explicitly. *"So you're predicting nothing from this at all. Let's write that down and test it."* Do not argue with the prediction — run the experiment.

**Prediction of P: 0 / M: 2**
A critical shift. The client still anticipates no enjoyment, but is allowing the possibility of some sense of achievement. The belief *"I am completely useless"* is already fracturing — even before any action has been taken.

> This non-zero mastery prediction is itself a clinical success. Document it explicitly in the SOAP Objective section. It indicates the intervention is beginning to work at the cognitive level.

**Prediction of P: 0 / M: 0 across multiple sessions with no movement**
Persistent zero predictions suggest the task may be too large, the reward too abstract, or an unaddressed maintenance mechanism is blocking engagement. Flag for task revision and formulation review. See [[behavioural_activation]].

## The Three Outcome Scenarios

After the activity, compare actual scores to predicted scores. The agent must handle three distinct scenarios:

### Scenario 1: Actual exceeds predicted
*Predicted M: 2 → Actual M: 6*

This is disconfirmatory evidence — the depression's prediction was wrong. This is a therapeutic moment, not just a data point. Flag explicitly for the therapist to name directly with the client.

**The intervention:** *"You predicted two. You got six. What do you make of that gap?"*

Do not move past this. The client's answer to that question is often the most clinically significant moment of the session. It is the first crack in the maintenance cycle's authority.

> **Agent instruction:** Flag Scenario 1 as **Disconfirmatory Evidence** in the SOAP Assessment. Quote the predicted and actual scores. Note the gap explicitly.

### Scenario 2: Actual matches predicted (low)
*Predicted M: 2 → Actual M: 2*

The prediction was accurate but the score remains low. This is not failure — it is information. The task may have been correctly calibrated but the reward structure needs examination.

**Clinical questions to consider:** Was the task too abstract? Too large despite appearing small? Was it completed in isolation when social context would have increased mastery? Did a maintenance mechanism intervene during the task?

> **Agent instruction:** Flag Scenario 2 as **Task Calibration Review Needed** in the SOAP Plan. Do not mark as treatment failure.

### Scenario 3: Actual below predicted
*Predicted M: 3 → Actual M: 0*

Something intervened between the prediction and the activity. Either the task was disrupted, a maintenance mechanism activated during the attempt, or the client is retrospectively downgrading the experience.

> **Agent instruction:** Flag Scenario 3 for explicit exploration in next session. Note in SOAP Plan: *"Discrepancy between predicted and actual mastery warrants direct exploration — identify what intervened."*

## Tracking Across Sessions

Single session ratings are snapshots. Clinical value accumulates across sessions.

**The trajectory to watch:**
- Mastery scores rising while pleasure remains flat = early recovery, expected pattern, continue BA
- Both scores rising together = mid-recovery, intervention working well
- Both scores flat or falling across three sessions = formulation review indicated
- Pleasure rising without mastery = atypical, worth noting, may indicate task selection skewed toward hedonic rather than achievement activities

> **Agent instruction:** Where multiple sessions of P/M data are present in a transcript or referenced historically, note the trajectory pattern in the SOAP Assessment. A single score means less than its direction of travel — the same principle as PHQ-9. See [[PHQ9_scoring]].

## The Clinical Error To Avoid

**The Happy Bias.**

Evaluating the success of a BA task based on Pleasure alone. This error is particularly common in less experienced therapists because pleasure feels like the intuitive measure of wellbeing.

A depressed client who completes a difficult task and reports P: 0, M: 5 has had a highly productive session. That Mastery score is counter-evidence to the core helplessness belief. Responding to it with *"but you didn't enjoy it much"* actively undermines the intervention.

Validate the Mastery first, always. The Pleasure follows.

## What The Agent Should Do With P/M Data

**1. Capture the prediction**
If a client references a BA task set in a previous session, look for the predicted scores. If they were not stated, note this as a gap — the experiment has no control condition without a baseline prediction.

**2. Capture the actual scores**
Extract post-activity ratings from the transcript. Note whether they were recorded immediately after the activity (valid) or recalled retrospectively at the next session (less reliable — memory in depression is negatively biased).

**3. Calculate and document the delta**
Compare predicted to actual for both metrics. Note the direction and magnitude.

**4. Classify the scenario**
Disconfirmatory Evidence / Task Calibration Review / Intervening Factor — and document accordingly in SOAP Assessment and Plan.

**5. Apply the Mastery First rule**
Never interpret low Pleasure in isolation. Always check Mastery before drawing conclusions about intervention efficacy.

**6. Document in SOAP**
- **Objective:** Raw predicted and actual scores, both metrics
- **Assessment:** Scenario classification, trajectory note, clinical interpretation
- **Plan:** Any task revision indicated, any therapeutic moment to revisit next session
